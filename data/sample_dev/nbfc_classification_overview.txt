NBFC SCALE-BASED REGULATION — DEV FIXTURE (simplified, not an official text)

1. The four layers
NBFCs are sorted into four regulatory layers based mainly on their size
and the kind of risk they carry:
- Base Layer (NBFC-BL): smaller, non-deposit-taking NBFCs below a set
  asset threshold, with lighter-touch regulation.
- Middle Layer (NBFC-ML): all deposit-taking NBFCs, plus non-deposit
  NBFCs above the Base Layer asset threshold.
- Upper Layer (NBFC-UL): the top NBFCs identified by RBI using a
  scoring methodology (size, interconnectedness, complexity); these
  face bank-like regulatory requirements.
- Top Layer (NBFC-TL): currently empty in practice, reserved for NBFCs
  that RBI believes pose an unacceptably high systemic risk even after
  Upper Layer treatment.

2. Why the tiering exists
The idea is proportionate regulation: a small NBFC lending in one city
shouldn't face the same compliance burden as a large, systemically
important one — but the largest NBFCs should face rules close to what
banks face, since their failure could affect the wider financial system.

3. Capital and governance
As an NBFC moves up a layer, requirements get stricter: higher capital
adequacy expectations, more board-level committees, tighter large
exposure limits, and closer supervisory reporting.

4. Annual review
RBI reviews which NBFCs sit in the Upper Layer every year, and an NBFC
can move between layers if its size or risk profile changes materially.
