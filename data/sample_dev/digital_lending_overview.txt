DIGITAL LENDING — DEV FIXTURE (simplified, not an official text)

1. Scope
This note applies to banks, co-operative banks, NBFCs (including housing
finance companies), and all-India financial institutions that lend money
through a digital app or website, whether they run the app themselves or
use an outside Lending Service Provider (LSP).

2. Lender-LSP relationship
Any lender working with an LSP needs a written contract spelling out who
is responsible for what. The lender stays fully on the hook for anything
the LSP does or fails to do on its behalf — the arrangement does not
shift legal responsibility away from the regulated lender.

3. Borrower protections
- The borrower's age, job, and income must be checked before any loan is
  approved; credit limits cannot increase automatically without consent.
- A short, plain-language summary of the loan's key terms (the "Key
  Facts Statement") must be given before the borrower signs.
- Loan money must go straight to the borrower's own bank account, and
  repayments must go straight back to the lender's account — the LSP app
  is not allowed to sit in the middle of the money flow.
- Borrowers get a minimum cooling-off window after taking the loan,
  during which they can back out without a penalty.

4. Data and privacy
- Apps may only collect data they actually need for the loan decision,
  and only with the borrower's clear consent.
- Apps are not allowed to read a borrower's phone contacts, files, or
  call logs.
- All borrower data has to be stored on servers located in India.

5. Guarantee arrangements (DLG)
When an LSP offers to cover some of a lender's losses on a loan pool
(a "default loss guarantee"), that cover cannot be larger than 5% of
the amount disbursed in that pool, and it can only be given as cash,
a fixed deposit, or a bank guarantee — not as some other informal
promise.
