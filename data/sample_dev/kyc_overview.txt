KYC / CUSTOMER DUE DILIGENCE — DEV FIXTURE (simplified, not an official text)

1. Why KYC matters
Banks and NBFCs must know who their customers really are, both when an
account is opened and on an ongoing basis, mainly to prevent money
laundering and the financing of illegal activity.

2. Periodic updation
Customer records are not a one-time exercise. Regulated entities must
periodically refresh KYC details:
- Low-risk individual customers: roughly every 10 years.
- Medium-risk customers: roughly every 8 years.
- High-risk customers: roughly every 2 years.
If a customer doesn't respond to an update request, the entity can
restrict the account's operation (for example, allowing credits but not
debits) until the update is done, rather than closing it outright.

3. Simplified updation
If nothing material has changed about the customer (address, business,
risk category), a simplified updation — a self-declaration rather than
fresh paperwork — is generally allowed for individual customers.

4. Inoperative accounts
Accounts with no customer-induced transactions for a long stretch (the
exact threshold is set by the entity's board, commonly around 2 years)
are reclassified as inoperative. Reactivating them requires the customer
to appear (in person or through video KYC) and complete fresh due
diligence, not just make a transaction.

5. Risk categorisation
Customers must be sorted into risk categories (low/medium/high) based on
factors like their location, type of business, and transaction pattern,
and this categorisation drives how often their KYC gets refreshed.
